**1. `FAIRNESS`, `LOCALITY`, and `F_L` are byte-identical to each other — and they hotspot badly.**
I checked instance-level routing, not just aggregate stats: in every scenario, these three policies route the _exact same sequence of requests to the exact same replicas_, even though their underlying signals (fairness debt vs. locality score) are genuinely different per request. In `balanced_control` all three dump 956/1000 requests onto instance 0 and starve the rest — goodput craters from ~9.85 req/s (RR/LOAD baseline) to ~0.6 req/s, TTFT p99 blows up to 7,500–45,000 ms. This isn't the algorithm being "bad at fairness/locality" — three different scoring functions can't legitimately produce identical argmax routing on every single request unless there's a shared bug (likely a normalization or tie-break defect in `router.py` that collapses both signals to a degenerate all-0/all-1 vector, after which some default tie-break always wins). **This is the single most actionable thing to debug before trusting any other result.**

**2. `H0` (linear) and `H1` (multiplicative) route identically in all 6 scenarios.**
Checked directly, not inferred from scores: every request goes to the same replica under both forms, in every scenario. This is actually a meaningful empirical result, not just a bug — it's consistent with the LMETRIC "Simple is Better" paper's condition for when linear ≈ multiplicative (one candidate dominates on all axes, so argmax is combination-form-invariant). If this holds up under deeper inspection, it's evidence _against_ your falsifiable hypothesis that combination form matters — worth checking whether your candidate set per request is too small/too dominated for the forms to ever disagree, since that would mean your ablation isn't actually testing what you think it's testing.

**3. `H4`, `FAIRROUTE`, `L_P`, and `F_L_P` also route identically everywhere.**
Since `FAIRROUTE` = `H4` by construction that's expected, but `L_P` (locality+prediction, _no fairness term_) matching them exactly suggests the fairness component isn't moving the argmax at all in your current weighting — the fairness signal may be getting drowned out by locality/prediction rather than acting as the "hard counter-weight" your design calls for.

**4. Jain's Index is completely insensitive to routing policy — it varies only by scenario.**
Every one of the 28 policies gets identical Jain's Index within a scenario (e.g., 0.1402 for all 28 in `real_workload_1000`). That's because it's computed over each client's _total completed output tokens_, which in a closed-loop replay is basically fixed by the workload script regardless of which replica served the request. **Your fairness metric as instrumented can't detect fairness violations from routing at all right now** — you likely need Jain's Index (or the VTC deficit) over a routing-sensitive quantity instead, like per-client mean latency, per-client queueing delay, or a time-windowed service-rate deficit.

**5. `H2`/`H3` (the gated/lexicographic-style forms) are the only ones that don't collapse under `full_adversarial`.**
There, `H2`/`H3` hit 9.08 goodput / 79.5% SLO — beating every prior-art baseline _and_ `FAIRROUTE`/`H4` (5.55 / 48.8%). If reproducible, that's a genuinely interesting result for your thesis: the gated form is more robust to adversarial conflict than the form you currently ship as FairRoute.

I'd fix #1 first (it's corrupting several other numbers downstream), then re-run the matrix before drawing conclusions from #3–#5.
